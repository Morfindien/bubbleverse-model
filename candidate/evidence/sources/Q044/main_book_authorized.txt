Numerical precision is not yet a cosmological result
An investigation of a frozen optical-depth integration component has established conditional mathematical
bounds on a specified spline-based functional. This is a useful numerical qualification, but it is not a new
measurement of the expansion rate, a validated correction to the cosmic microwave background, or a comparison
of cosmological models.
The relevant calculation distinguishes the native implementation of an optical-depth functional from an ordinary
cubic integral. Whether that difference changes the physical reionization history, predicted CMB temperature and
polarization spectra, or the likelihood of a cosmological model remains unmeasured in the qualified original
analysis.
Therefore the existing Hubble-constant benchmarks and the cautious assessment of early dark energy remain
unchanged. Neither equivalence between the CamSpec and HiLLiPoP inference pipelines nor a decisive
difference between them has been established by this result.
