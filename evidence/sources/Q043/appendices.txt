Component and Coordinate Localization
The independently implemented HiLLiPoP calculation remains a valid numerical result. All
nine mapped-start optimizations and the required tighter continuations completed, and no
reproducibly supported stable basin was found under the preregistered seven-coordinate
common-geometry graph classifier. Several HiLLiPoP endpoints nevertheless remained
widely separated in common-parameter space despite having similar objective values, so
the result should not be confused with convergence to one identical solution.
The original portability interpretation relied heavily on comparing this result with the
historical CamSpec endpoint family, which had been classified as stable multibasin
structure. That comparison was subsequently shown not to use a common operational
definition. The historical CamSpec classification used a per-mask geometry combining
cosmological and nuisance-parameter separations, whereas the later analysis used a seven-
coordinate common-geometry graph classifier. Applying both definitions to the same
preserved CamSpec endpoint sets removes the apparent binary contradiction: all of the
tested CamSpec endpoint sets are classified as stable under the historical rule and non-
stable under the later graph rule. The stable-versus-non-stable distinction is therefore
classifier dependent and cannot by itself establish cross-implementation non-portability.
The surrounding isolation tests remain informative. Restoring the omitted TT support, TE,
and EE individually, pairwise, and together did not produce repeated stable clusters under
the later classifier. Reconstruction of the executed parameter references showed that both
relevant CamSpec paths already used scalar pointlike references, excluding scalar-versus-
distribution launch semantics as the explanation. Fixing n_s, log A, and tau_reio inside the
later full-native construction likewise failed to create repeated stable clusters under that
classifier. These results constrain the tested implementations, but they do not restore the
historical binary basin label as a classifier-invariant physical property.
The remaining portability question can be addressed without relying on the binary basin
label. A deterministic comparison was therefore made directly in the seven coordinates
shared by the CamSpec and HiLLiPoP constructions: ωb, ωc d m, f E D E, log10 zc, θi,sc f, H 0, and
A Pl anc k. Each coordinate was normalized by a scale fixed before the comparison, and the
inherited materiality threshold was held at 0.10.
The first continuous comparison revealed a large cross-implementation difference. All nine
matched CamSpec–HiLLiPoP endpoint pairs exceeded the materiality threshold, with a
median normalized displacement of 2.3623. The normalized centroid displacement was
1.5371. The internal shapes of the endpoint families also differed: all 36 pairwise-distance
drifts exceeded 0.10, with a median drift of 2.2590. The historical stable-versus-non-stable
distinction was therefore not a valid common-classifier argument, but removing that
binary label did not make the underlying fitted geometries equivalent.
A stronger control then asked whether the continuous difference was primarily caused by
the two implementations using different temperature-spectrum information. Instead of
generating another set of likelihood solutions, the analysis reused the already validated
paired CamSpec and HiLLiPoP refinement endpoints obtained on exactly matched TT
support. The common support retained the 143×143 spectrum over 50≤ℓ≤2000 and the 143×217 and 217×217 spectra over
500≤ℓ≤2500. The same seven coordinates, the same
locked normalization scales, and the same 0.10 materiality threshold were preserved.
The implementation difference survived this restriction. All nine matched endpoint
displacements again exceeded 0.10, with a median normalized displacement of
2.9317906966 and a maximum of 5.2295437935. The normalized centroid displacement was
0.8311487237. All 36 pairwise geometry drifts also remained above threshold, with a
median of 1.6367910228 and a maximum of 4.7555726067. Independent numerical replay
reproduced the derived result with a maximum reported difference of zero.
This materially weakens a simple explanation in which the previously observed
displacement is caused mainly by unequal TT information support. It does not show that
information support can never matter, but the exact common-support restriction actually
tested here does not remove either the matched-location difference or the change in
internal endpoint-family shape.
The scientifically defensible conclusion is therefore specific. Under the tested n=3 early-
dark-energy construction, CamSpec and HiLLiPoP produce materially different fitted
parameter geometries, and that difference persists when their TT information support is
explicitly matched. The surviving discrepancy is therefore more plausibly associated with
some remaining aspect of likelihood implementation or multidimensional model
construction than with the tested support mismatch alone.
A further preregistered localization test asked whether this surviving difference could
nevertheless be reduced to a small subset of the seven common coordinates,
ωb,ωc d m, F E D E,log10 zc,θi,sc f , H 0, A Pl anc k .
All 127 non-empty coordinate subsets were evaluated symmetrically using the same locked
normalization scales. To qualify, a subset had to capture at least 80% of the full matched-
location, centroid, and pairwise-shape signal and remain robust under leave-one-label-out
tests. No one-, two-, or three-coordinate subset satisfied the complete criterion. The unique
smallest qualifying subset contained four coordinates,
ωb,ωc d m, F E D E,θi,sc f .
This four-dimensional subset captured 90.89% of the matched-location signal, 88.48% of the
centroid displacement, and 87.20% of the pairwise-shape difference. It also passed all nine
leave-one-label-out tests in each of the three geometric channels. The surviving CamSpec–
HiLLiPoP difference is therefore distributed across a reproducible multivariate parameter
structure rather than concentrated in one or two common coordinates.
The decomposition also shows why a simple single-parameter narrative is inadequate.
Different coordinates dominate different aspects of the geometry. The matched-location
and pairwise-shape differences receive their largest individual contributions from f E D E,
while the centroid displacement is dominated by ωc d m and θi,sc f. H 0, A Pl anc k, and log10 zc are
not required in the minimal robust subset. This weakens explanations in which the
implementation difference is fundamentally an H 0-only or A Pl anc k-only effect.
The result is nevertheless a localization in fitted parameter space, not a localization of
physical causality. It does not show that ωb, ωc d m, f E D E, or θi,sc f is itself responsible for the
discrepancy. Possible implementation-level contributors still include foreground treatment,
calibration structure, nuisance-parameter semantics, covariance treatment, likelihood
construction, or coupled effects among these ingredients.
This remains a methodological result rather than a new observation of the sky. CamSpec
and HiLLiPoP analyse overlapping Planck PR4/NPIPE information, so their disagreement
cannot be counted as independent observational evidence. The calculation does not
determine which implementation is more accurate, establish a Planck calibration or
foreground error, demonstrate a covariance defect or instrumental failure, favour or
falsify early dark energy, alter the active H 0 benchmarks, or provide evidence for new
physics.

Morfindien/bubbleverse-model is the version-controlled repository for the accepted,
candidate and formal Bubbleverse model state. It is distinct from Morfindien/Bubbleverse,
