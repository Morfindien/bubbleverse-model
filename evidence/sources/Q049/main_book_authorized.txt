What the new result establishes
A focused analysis now places a finite upper bound on the remaining low-redshift difference in Thomson
optical depth for the frozen hillipop-ede_n3 configuration. Thomson depth describes the accumulated
scattering opacity from free electrons; it is dimensionless. The comparison holds the accepted
reionization redshift fixed and contrasts residual helium held at q = 10-6 with a reset to q = 0 after the
same event.
|ΔτT,tail| ≤ 46.67050453,   0 ≤ z ≤ 810
The result applies to existing admitted source-real paired continuations that reach z = 0, under
the frozen inputs and common reionization prescription. “Source-real” denotes the mathematical
continuum model defined from the pinned code; it does not certify the native floating-point trajectory.
Why a finite bound is not a small correction
The bound is deliberately very coarse. It allows an electron-fraction difference of order unity and
therefore does not show that the actual effect is small. It determines neither the sign nor a positive
lower bound on that difference; zero remains possible. The narrow numerical enclosure of the
upper-bound formula is not an error bar on a measured physical effect.
The finite bound closes the narrower Q049 question within its stated assumptions. It does not complete
the four-configuration electron-history and cumulative-depth comparison in Q048, prove that the
required continuations exist uniquely, or qualify a native table or spline.
